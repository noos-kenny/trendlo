# 09 — Creative Examples Library

A working library of concrete creative concepts mapped to triggers. Use as inspiration during hypothesis design and briefing — not as templates to copy.

Each example is structured as: **Format · Hook · Mid · Close · Why it works · Best stage**.

The brand context is *Nordic premium fashion / lifestyle* unless noted. Adapt voice and aesthetic to the specific brand.

---

## Cluster 1 — Risk & Trust Reduction

### Risk Reversal — concept 1: "The 100-day fit promise"

- **Format.** 15s static carousel with one hero card, three product cards.
- **Hook.** Card 1: A clean black card, white type. *"100 days to know."*
- **Mid.** Card 2–4: product, named cuts.
- **Close.** Card 5: *"Wear them. Wash them. Live in them. If they don't earn their place, send them back."*
- **Why it works.** The guarantee is the hero, not the product. The promise sets up the product as the consequence — and the time-frame (100 days, vs the standard 30) signals confidence.
- **Best stage.** BOF, cart-abandoners and recent PDP visitors.

### Risk Reversal — concept 2: "The founder's promise"

- **Format.** 30s talking-head video.
- **Hook.** *"Hi — I'm [founder]. I'll keep this short."*
- **Mid.** Two sentences on the product. One on the guarantee. *"If they're not the best you've owned, I'll buy them back."*
- **Close.** Cut to product, simple end card.
- **Why it works.** Personal accountability strengthens the guarantee. The founder is the proof.
- **Best stage.** MOF → BOF.

### Authority Framing — concept 3: "Tested at —"

- **Format.** 10s product video with overlay.
- **Hook.** *"Tested at SP Sveriges Tekniska Forskningsinstitut."* on a still product shot.
- **Mid.** Three quick frames showing test environments.
- **Close.** *"100 wash cycles. The colour does not move."*
- **Why it works.** Specific Nordic-credible authority lands hard with Nordic audiences. Single, falsifiable claim.
- **Best stage.** MOF.

### Transparency — concept 4: "What €280 actually pays for"

- **Format.** 30s video, infographic style, founder voiceover.
- **Hook.** *"Most brands won't show you this."*
- **Mid.** Cost breakdown bar: materials, labour, freight, brand margin, retail margin if relevant.
- **Close.** *"Here's where your money goes. We think you should know."*
- **Why it works.** Transparency as content. Particularly strong in Nordic markets where audiences read price as a signal of honesty.
- **Best stage.** MOF; secondary use in cold prospecting for design-literate audiences (DK, NO).

## Cluster 2 — Motivation & Identity

### Identity Signaling — concept 5: "After ten years"

- **Format.** 60s documentary-style portrait.
- **Hook.** Open on a hand reaching into a wardrobe. Cut to subject (architect, 34, in her own apartment). *"After ten years of cheaper denim, I stopped pretending fit was the same as feel."*
- **Mid.** B-roll of the subject in her life — coffee, studio, evening — wearing the brand's flagship cut.
- **Close.** No CTA. End card with brand mark.
- **Why it works.** Identity disqualifies as much as it qualifies. The line filters the audience to the segment that converts.
- **Best stage.** Prospecting on lookalike-of-customer audiences.

### Identity Signaling — concept 6: "Three Stockholms"

- **Format.** 90s film, mosaic of three subjects across a Stockholm day.
- **Hook.** Sound only — a city waking. First subject in a Södermalm flat.
- **Mid.** Subject A puts on a coat, walks to the studio. Subject B at a kitchen table. Subject C on a tram. Each subject's environment is distinct; the only constant is a piece of the brand.
- **Close.** Title: *"For people who get dressed for their own day."* Brand mark.
- **Why it works.** Compounded identity signal — it is *not one identity*, it is the cluster of identities the brand serves.
- **Best stage.** Brand-equity prospecting; low expectation on direct response.

### Aspiration — concept 7: "The morning you've been imagining"

- **Format.** 20s vertical video.
- **Hook.** Slow pan across an apartment in cool morning light — empty, calm.
- **Mid.** Subject enters frame, makes coffee, opens window. Wearing the brand's loungewear.
- **Close.** Title: *"Before the day starts."* Brand mark.
- **Why it works.** Aspirational without being unattainable. The aspiration is *the morning*, not a lifestyle the buyer cannot reach.
- **Best stage.** Prospecting on lifestyle-aligned audiences.

### Commitment & Consistency — concept 8: "Find your fit"

- **Format.** Interactive quiz creative (Meta lead form or click-to-microsite).
- **Hook.** Static creative: *"Two questions. Then a fit that's actually yours."*
- **Mid.** Quiz: body type, preferred rise, preferred wash. Six combinations.
- **Close.** Personalised result page with three recommendations and a 100-day fit guarantee.
- **Why it works.** The quiz is a small commitment that aligns the buyer with the brand. The personalised result is now *theirs*.
- **Best stage.** MOF → BOF transition.

## Cluster 3 — Social & Proof Mechanics

### Volume Social Proof — concept 9: "Three drops, three sell-throughs"

- **Format.** 10s static.
- **Hook.** *"Drop 01: sold out in 14 hours."*
- **Mid.** *"Drop 02: 9 hours. Drop 03: 4 hours."*
- **Close.** Product image. *"Drop 04 — Friday at noon."*
- **Why it works.** Sequential proof of scarcity, demonstrated by past behaviour. Each subsequent drop is more credible.
- **Best stage.** Drop launch (combined Scarcity + Volume Proof).

### Similarity Proof — concept 10: "Why Anna switched"

- **Format.** 45s UGC-style video, single subject, vertical.
- **Hook.** Subject in casual setting, talking to camera. *"I'd been buying [adjacent competitor] for years. Then I tried these."*
- **Mid.** Subject describes one specific point of difference in her own words. Shows the product on her body.
- **Close.** *"I've bought three more pairs since."* Brand mark.
- **Why it works.** Specific switching narrative + named competitor (where regulatorily safe; otherwise implied) outperforms generic praise.
- **Best stage.** MOF on look-alike-of-competitor-customer audiences.

### Transformation Proof — concept 11: "One closet, three weeks"

- **Format.** 30s before/after video.
- **Hook.** Wardrobe before — overstuffed, disordered.
- **Mid.** Time-lapse text: "Week 1 / 2 / 3" with corresponding wardrobe states.
- **Close.** Wardrobe after — six pieces, all named. *"You don't need more. You need better."*
- **Why it works.** Transformation reframed as *reduction*. Especially powerful in Nordic markets where excess is suspect.
- **Best stage.** MOF; works as long-form on owned channels and shortened for retargeting.

## Cluster 4 — Attention & Cognitive Bias

### Loss Aversion — concept 12: "The size 36 problem"

- **Format.** 6s vertical video.
- **Hook.** Tight on a denim label reading "36." *"There are 11 left."*
- **Mid.** Brief cut to the cut on the model. *"They will not be re-stocked."*
- **Close.** *"Now or never."*
- **Why it works.** Real scarcity, single size, no manufactured urgency. The Loss Aversion frame is honest.
- **Best stage.** BOF retargeting on size-specific viewers.

### Curiosity / Open Loop — concept 13: "We almost killed this"

- **Format.** 30s talking-head video.
- **Hook.** Founder, mid-sentence: *"We almost killed this product six months in."*
- **Mid.** Brief story of the issue (a sourcing problem, a fit problem, a price problem) and what was done about it.
- **Close.** *"Glad we didn't."* Cut to product.
- **Why it works.** Open loop drives 3-second view rate. The resolution is honest, not bait.
- **Best stage.** Prospecting; secondary use as MOF for the brand-curious.

### Contrast & Anchoring — concept 14: "What you'd usually pay"

- **Format.** 15s split-screen video.
- **Hook.** Left: a similar competitor product (where shown safely or implied). Right: the brand's product.
- **Mid.** Type appears: *"Same fabric weight."* Then: *"Same factory."* Then: *"Half the markup."*
- **Close.** Price reveal with subtle context.
- **Why it works.** Anchoring to a known reference point makes the price feel definitively reasonable.
- **Best stage.** BOF, especially against price objections from cart-abandoners.

## Layered concepts (two triggers in one asset)

### Identity + Authority — concept 15: "She designed it for herself first"

- **Format.** 60s film.
- **Hook.** Designer, in her studio, on the phone. Cuts of pattern, fabric, fitting.
- **Mid.** Voiceover: *"I made this because I couldn't find what I wanted. The first sample fitted me. Now it fits other people too."*
- **Close.** Subject wearing the piece, leaving the studio.
- **Why it works.** Cultural Authority (the designer) layered with Identity Signaling (the audience recognises her as one of theirs).
- **Best stage.** Brand-equity prospecting on tastemaker lookalike audiences.

### Curiosity + Risk Reversal — concept 16: "Try it for free, basically"

- **Format.** 15s static carousel.
- **Hook.** Card 1: *"There's only one way to know if these are your jeans."*
- **Mid.** Card 2: *"Wear them. For 100 days."* Card 3: *"In real life. To work, to dinner, in the rain."*
- **Close.** Card 4: *"If they're not yours, send them back. We pay."*
- **Why it works.** Curiosity opens the loop, Risk Reversal closes it. The CTA is implicit — try them.
- **Best stage.** BOF.

### Volume Proof + Loss Aversion — concept 17: "9,800 chose this. 23 left."

- **Format.** 6s static.
- **Hook.** Number, large: *"9,800."* Subtitle: *"women in Scandinavia have chosen this fit."*
- **Mid.** Cut: *"23 left in your size."*
- **Close.** Brand mark, subtle CTA.
- **Why it works.** Consensus + scarcity in two beats. Honesty depends on the numbers being true.
- **Best stage.** BOF retargeting on size-specific viewers.

## Anti-examples

A small list of patterns we deliberately avoid, with the reason.

- **Manufactured countdown timers on continuity products.** Damages premium positioning, trains the audience to wait.
- **Stacked influencer testimonials with no specific claim.** Reads as paid social, converts as paid social.
- **"Limited time only" overlaid on always-available SKUs.** The audience reads through it within months.
- **Stylised before/after where the after looks unattainable.** Lifts CTR, suppresses CVR, raises return rate.
- **Authority claims with logos the Nordic audience doesn't revere.** A US business publication carries less weight than a named Nordic peer-press credit.

## How to use this library

1. Treat as inspiration, not template. Each concept needs to be re-rendered in the specific brand's voice and aesthetic.
2. Use during hypothesis design — "we want to try concept 5 for this account, applied to outerwear" is a legitimate starting point. Then write the hypothesis card.
3. Add to the library as new patterns prove out. Maintain a similar structure (format, hook, mid, close, why, stage) for additions.
