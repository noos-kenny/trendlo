# 10 — Anti-Patterns & Pitfalls

A working list of failure modes we have observed — in our own work and in audits of others — and what to do instead. Use as a pre-launch checklist and a quarterly review prompt.

The patterns are organised by where they originate: hypothesis-level, creative-level, media-level, organisational, and ethical.

## Hypothesis-level anti-patterns

### 1. The unfalsifiable hypothesis

**Symptom.** A hypothesis card whose predicted outcome is "may improve performance" or "is expected to resonate."

**Why it fails.** No result disconfirms it. Nothing is learned regardless of what happens.

**Do instead.** Force directional and magnitude prediction. Even a wrong prediction produces a learning when written down before the test.

### 2. The retrofit

**Symptom.** A hypothesis written *after* the result is known, or quietly edited once the data comes in.

**Why it fails.** The system collapses into rationalisation. Worse, it pretends to be a learning organisation while actually being one that confirms its priors.

**Do instead.** Write hypotheses to a timestamped ledger before launch. Treat post-hoc edits as a process violation.

### 3. The audience too broad

**Symptom.** A hypothesis served to "all women 25–45 interested in fashion."

**Why it fails.** The segment is too heterogeneous to share an objection, and the result averages over too many sub-realities to be interpretable.

**Do instead.** Define audiences by *behaviour* (cart-abandoners, repeat buyers, lookalikes of churned customers, recent PDP visitors) until the segment shares an objection.

### 4. The trigger-stage mismatch

**Symptom.** Risk Reversal at prospecting. Curiosity at BOF. Authority Framing in the first 1.5 seconds.

**Why it fails.** The trigger is doing a job the buyer isn't yet asking for, or no longer asking for.

**Do instead.** Cross-reference every hypothesis against `03_funnel_mapping.md` before approval. Trigger-stage validation is a non-negotiable step.

### 5. The aesthetic-only brief

**Symptom.** "Make it feel like Acne in 2018" with no underlying psychological objective.

**Why it fails.** The asset has no falsifiable prediction, so its result teaches nothing.

**Do instead.** Aesthetic references are constraints, not briefs. Pair every aesthetic reference with a hypothesis card.

## Creative-level anti-patterns

### 6. Trigger stacking

**Symptom.** An asset with five proof points, three different CTAs, and two distinct emotional registers.

**Why it fails.** Unreadable in the first second. The viewer's mind cannot process simultaneous contradictory persuasion strategies.

**Do instead.** One primary trigger per asset. Optionally one secondary, only if the two compose naturally.

### 7. The polished UGC

**Symptom.** Influencer or actor performing UGC aesthetics, but produced with full polish.

**Why it fails.** The audience reads it as an ad pretending to be UGC. The Similarity Proof trigger is destroyed by the inauthenticity.

**Do instead.** Either commit to UGC and accept lower production polish, or make a fully produced asset and own that.

### 8. The over-stylised before/after

**Symptom.** Transformation Proof asset where the "after" looks airbrushed or staged.

**Why it fails.** Lifts CTR, suppresses CVR, raises return rate — buyers expect what they were sold and feel deceived when they don't get it.

**Do instead.** Identical lighting and framing in before/after. Honest representation, even at the cost of less dramatic visual contrast.

### 9. The borrowed-authority logo wall

**Symptom.** "As seen in" with logos the target audience does not actually revere.

**Why it fails.** Authority is a relationship between the source and the audience. Using sources the audience doesn't recognise wastes the trigger.

**Do instead.** Audit authority sources for local credibility. In Nordic markets, this often means swapping US press credits for Nordic peer-press, named experts, or design-canon references.

### 10. The manufactured countdown

**Symptom.** "Ends tonight" copy on continuity products that will continue to be sold tomorrow.

**Why it fails.** Erodes premium positioning, trains the audience to wait, and damages brand long-term for short-term lift.

**Do instead.** Use Loss Aversion only where the constraint is real. Drops, last sizes, time-bounded offers — all valid. Recurring "last chance" emails are not.

### 11. The everywhere-the-same ad

**Symptom.** A single creative concept served identically across prospecting, MOF, and BOF.

**Why it fails.** The same trigger is doing different jobs at different stages. One asset cannot do all three jobs without underperforming on at least two.

**Do instead.** Stage-specific creative. Even if the *concept* is shared, the cut, the CTA, and the emphasis should differ.

### 12. The casting cliché

**Symptom.** Identity Signaling creative cast with people who read as professional models, not as the audience.

**Why it fails.** The trigger relies on the viewer recognising themselves or their tribe. A model in branded fit signals "this is an ad," not "this is for me."

**Do instead.** Cast real customers, micro-tastemakers, or professionals who carry the identity outside of fashion.

### 13. The fabricated first-person quote

A hook in quote form requires a real human behind the words. Fabricated first-person stories feel strong but are dishonest until they are true — and they risk slipping into production unnoticed. The same discipline applies to mockups in decks: ratings, quotes, and statistics in example ads must match the actual source material. Rule: quote/story formats are tagged "requires real source" and parked until the material exists; every number and quote in a mockup is fact-checked against the audit base before delivery.

### 14. The hook that fails the second read

Hooks that sound right on first read can fail on the second — linguistically (wrong subject: "your well isn't tested, just used to it" — a well cannot be 'used to' anything) or logically (claiming more than is true). The client, the Meta comment field, and legal always read twice. Rule: read every hook as a sceptic and ask two questions — "is the claim literally true?" and "can the sentence be misread?" Only then does it go into a deck or production.

## Media-level anti-patterns

### 15. Spend-skewed pattern detection

**Symptom.** A trigger pattern analysis that concludes "Identity Signaling wins" because the winning Identity Signaling asset received 5× the spend of any other asset.

**Why it fails.** Causality runs the other way. The asset got more spend *because* it was winning, not the other way around.

**Do instead.** Normalise pattern attribution by spend cohort. Compare assets at similar spend levels.

### 16. Audience saturation read as creative fatigue

**Symptom.** CTR declines, the team blames the creative, refreshes it, CTR continues to decline.

**Why it fails.** The constraint was the audience's frequency, not the asset.

**Do instead.** Track new vs returning impressions alongside CTR. Refresh creative when CTR decays *against* a stable audience composition.

### 17. The single-channel creative system

**Symptom.** Hypotheses tested only on Meta. Performance on Google, TikTok, programmatic invisible.

**Why it fails.** Triggers and audiences behave differently across channels. The pattern bank built on one channel does not generalise cleanly.

**Do instead.** Cross-channel hypothesis testing where budget allows. Tag and analyse separately.

## Organisational anti-patterns

### 18. The hypothesis ledger that nobody updates

**Symptom.** Hypotheses written into a system, results never logged, learnings never harvested.

**Why it fails.** The system performs Creative Intelligence without doing it. The compounding learning never accrues.

**Do instead.** Make the ledger a non-optional part of the cadence. Retros every fortnight. The strategist is responsible for the learning entry, not the analyst.

### 19. Creative and media in separate rooms

**Symptom.** Creative team writes briefs without media context. Media team optimises bidding with no view into the hypothesis the asset is testing.

**Why it fails.** The principle in `01_creative_intelligence_system.md` collapses. Without shared structure, the system reverts to creative production + media optimisation.

**Do instead.** One document per hypothesis with audience, stage, trigger, angle, proof, and predicted KPI. Both teams own it jointly.

### 20. Brand and performance in separate rooms

**Symptom.** Brand work runs to a different vocabulary, calendar, and review chain than performance work.

**Why it fails.** The brand voice doesn't make it into performance creative; performance learnings don't influence brand strategy. Both halves underperform their potential.

**Do instead.** A short performance-creative voice extension to the brand book, owned by the brand team. Performance work reviewed by brand at first-asset-per-trigger; subsequent assets approved at lower depth.

### 21. AI as a content firehose

**Symptom.** AI generating volumes of unconnected creative variants, none of which are testing a hypothesis.

**Why it fails.** Variety is not testing. Volume of unconnected creative produces noise, not learning.

**Do instead.** AI scales *within* a hypothesis once it is established. Use AI for variation production, not primary hypothesis generation.

## Ethical anti-patterns

These are not just commercial mistakes. They damage long-term equity, occasionally damage individual customers, and increasingly run afoul of platform and regulatory enforcement.

### 22. False scarcity

Using scarcity language ("only 3 left") on products that are not actually scarce. Damages trust on detection. Increasingly enforceable as misleading commercial practice (EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, Konsumentverket guidance).

### 23. Body-shaming framings in transformation creative

Implying the "before" state is inadequate. Damaging to vulnerable audiences and increasingly disallowed by Meta and TikTok policy.

### 24. Manipulated reviews and proof

Inflated review counts, undisclosed paid testimonials, screenshots of stars that are not the actual rating. Beyond unethical — illegal under Nordic consumer protection law and EU directives.

### 25. Health and outcome claims without proof

Implying outcomes (weight loss, skin clearing, mood improvement) without substantiated evidence. Risk areas include skincare, supplements, wellness, fitness apparel.

### 26. Targeting vulnerability

Using audiences defined by signals of distress, financial vulnerability, or mental health condition. Rules vary by platform; the ethical line is upstream of the platform rule.

The simple test: if the trigger only works because the audience is vulnerable, the trigger is the wrong one.

## Pre-launch checklist

A short checklist applied to every paid creative before launch. Five items, two minutes per asset:

1. Can I name the primary trigger in one word?
2. Can I name the audience and stage?
3. Can I complete the *because…* sentence?
4. Is the prediction written down and timestamped?
5. Does the asset avoid all 26 anti-patterns above?

If yes to all five, ship. If no to any, fix or kill before spending.
