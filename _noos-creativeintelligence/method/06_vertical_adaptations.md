# 06 — Vertical Adaptations

The 15 triggers are vertical-agnostic at the level of mechanism (see `02_psychological_trigger_taxonomy.md`). What changes per vertical is the **expression**, the **weighting**, and the **objections** the buyer brings. This module maps the framework onto the business models NOOS works with.

For market-level (SE/NO/DK/FI) adaptation, see `05_nordic_market_psychology.md`. The two layers stack: pick the vertical weighting first, then calibrate for the market.

## E-commerce (B2C / D2C, non-subscription)

**Objection focus:** Price, fit, quality, regret risk, identity match.

**Trigger winners:** Risk Reversal (BOF), Identity Signaling (TOF), Similarity Proof (UGC), Transformation Proof (category-dependent), Loss Aversion (drops, real scarcity).

**KPI stack:** CTR, CVR, AOV, ROAS, return rate.

**What you need to know before setting strategy:** Hero products, entry products, drop roadmap, return rate per product.

## D2C subscription

**Objection focus:** Lock-in risk, value over time, churn fatigue from past subscriptions.

**Trigger winners:** Risk Reversal (pause anytime, money-back), Transparency (real customers walking through what actually arrives), Commitment & Consistency (quiz → trial), Identity Signaling (membership framing).

**KPI stack:** Trial-start rate, month-2 retention, LTV/CAC, churn.

**Trap:** Optimising for trial starts without tracking retention. Cheap trials that churn fast are worse than fewer trials that stay.

## B2B lead gen / B2B SaaS

**Objection focus:** Implementation cost, internal alignment, vendor risk, "another tool" fatigue.

**Trigger winners:** Authority Framing (analyst recognition, named customer logos at the right tier), Identity Signaling ("we're a modern data team — we use tools like X"), Transparency (public pricing where the category hides it), Similarity Proof (named peer companies).

**KPI stack:** CTR, CPL, MQL→SQL conversion, demo booking rate, demo→close rate, sales cycle length.

**Trap:** Optimising for the click, not the deal. Cheap MQLs that never close are worse.

**Watch out:** Risk Reversal aimed at already-warm audiences reads as desperation. Authority from the wrong tier of logos misfires.

## B2C lead gen (insurance, education, home services, financial)

**Objection focus:** Trust in the provider, complexity of the offering, fear of being upsold.

**Trigger winners:** Authority Framing (industry certifications, regulator approvals), Transparency (disclosure of how leads are handled), Risk Reversal ("no obligation quote"), Volume Social Proof (local customer numbers).

**KPI stack:** CTR, form completion rate, CPL, lead quality score (closed-loop with sales).

**Trap:** Form completion rate as the only KPI ignores lead quality.

## Quick reference — trigger weighting per vertical

| Vertical | Top-3 triggers (typically) | Avoid / use with care |
|---|---|---|
| Premium fashion | Identity Signaling, Cultural Authority, Scarcity (real) | Manufactured Loss Aversion, Volume Social Proof |
| Mass-market e-com | Volume Social Proof, Loss Aversion, Transformation Proof | Identity Signaling (too narrow) |
| D2C subscription | Risk Reversal, Transparency, Commitment | Loss Aversion (damages trust) |
| B2B SaaS | Authority, Identity Signaling, Similarity Proof | Risk Reversal at warm audiences |
| B2C lead gen | Authority, Transparency, Risk Reversal | Curiosity (disqualifies the wrong people) |
| Beauty / skincare | Transformation Proof, Authority (dermatologist), Similarity | Over-styled before/after |
| Outdoor / lifestyle | Identity Signaling, Aspiration, Cultural Authority | Generic aspirational |
